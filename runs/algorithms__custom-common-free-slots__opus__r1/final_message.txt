I've implemented `commonFreeSlots` in `solution.ts`, and it gives the right answers on a few quick checks.

**How it works:**
1. Every busy interval is cut down to the part inside the day, and any that end up empty are dropped.
2. The remaining intervals are sorted by start time, using an index array so 200,000 intervals sort quickly.
3. One pass then tracks the earliest time not yet covered by a busy interval. When the next busy interval starts later than that, the gap between them is a maximal free slot, and it's kept if it's at least `minLength` long. Any free time between the last busy interval and `dayEnd` is checked the same way.

Overall it runs in O(N log N) for N busy intervals and uses no imports.

**Results of the checks:**
- The example from the spec returns `[[5,8]]`.
- With no people, the whole day `[[0,10]]` is returned.
- With people who have no busy intervals and `minLength` longer than the day, it returns `[]`.
- Touching intervals `[1,3]` and `[3,5]` leave no gap between them, giving `[[0,1],[5,10]]`.
- A busy interval covering the whole day, starting before it and ending after it, returns `[]`.
