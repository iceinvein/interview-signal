I've written `rainWaterWithWalls` in `solution.ts`, and it passes the sample cases I ran.

**How it works:**
1. It goes through the bars from right to left and saves, for each bar, the tallest height from that bar to the end, starting from `rightWall`.
2. It then goes from left to right keeping the tallest height so far, starting from `leftWall`. For each bar, the water level is the lower of those two heights, and the water held is that level minus the bar's height.

This takes O(n) time and O(n) extra memory. The largest possible total is about 10¹⁰, which a JavaScript number holds exactly. There are no imports.

**Results (run with `tsx`):**

| Input | Output |
|---|---|
| `[]`, walls 5/5 | 0 |
| `[0,1,0,2,1,0,1,3,2,1,2,1]`, both sides open | 6 (matches the known answer for this standard example) |
| `[0,0,0]`, walls 2/3 | 6 |
| `[1,0]`, walls 0/5 | 1 |
| `[0]`, walls 3/0 | 0 (an open side holds no water) |

The last three are cases I worked out by hand to check the walls, and the outputs agree.
