I've implemented `shortestTeleportPath` in `solution.ts`. It's a plain breadth-first search, which finds the shortest path here because every move costs 1. Each cell has up to five neighbours: its four orthogonal neighbours, plus its twin if it's a portal. The file has no imports.

**Checks I ran:**
- `["Sa#a.E"]` gives 4 (the example)
- `["S#E"]` gives -1
- `["SE"]` gives 1
- `["S","E"]` gives 1
- `["S.a","###","a.E"]` gives 5 (the only route uses the portal)
- An open 1000×1000 grid with `S` and `E` in opposite corners gives 1998, in about 15 ms

**How it works:**
- It reads the grid once into flat typed arrays. While doing that, it pairs each portal letter with its twin by remembering where the letter first appeared.
- The queue is a pre-allocated `Int32Array`, which keeps memory and speed steady at the maximum size of 10⁶ cells.
- It returns as soon as it reaches `E`. It also returns -1 if `S` or `E` is missing, as a safety check.
