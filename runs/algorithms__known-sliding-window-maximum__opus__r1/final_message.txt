I've written `maxSlidingWindow` in `solution.ts` using a monotonic deque, so it runs in O(n) time and uses O(n) memory.

**How it works:** The deque holds positions in the array whose values get smaller from front to back.
- Before adding a new position, it drops any positions at the back whose values are less than or equal to the new value, since they can never be a block's largest again.
- It removes the front position once it falls outside the current block.
- The front of the deque then always holds the largest value in the current block.

The deque is a preallocated `Int32Array` with head and tail pointers, which avoids the slowdown of `Array.prototype.shift()`.

**Testing:** It gave the correct results for three hand-picked cases, including the standard example `[1,3,-1,-3,5,3,6,7], k=3 → [3,3,5,5,6,7]`. It also matched a simple brute-force version on 500 random inputs.
