I wrote `mergeIntervals` in `solution.ts`. I ran it on one mixed example and on an empty list, and both gave the right answer: `[[2,3],[1,2],[5,6],[5,6],[8,10],[9,9]]` → `[[1,3],[5,6],[8,10]]`, and `[]` → `[]`. I haven't tested anything beyond those two cases.

How it works:
- It sorts a copy of the input by `start`, so the caller's array isn't changed.
- It goes through the sorted intervals once. If an interval's `start` is less than or equal to the current interval's `end`, the two are merged. Using "less than or equal" means intervals that only touch at an end, like `[1,2]` and `[2,3]`, also combine.
- The merge only extends the `end`, so an interval that sits inside another (`[9,9]` inside `[8,10]`) or an exact duplicate is absorbed without changing anything.
- It takes O(n log n) time because of the sort, which is fine for 100,000 intervals.
- There are no imports.
